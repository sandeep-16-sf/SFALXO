Chapter 4: The Age of Industrialisation

Class 10 History Notes

I. Before Industrial Revolution & Hand Labour

Proto-Industrialisation: A phase of large-scale industrial production for an international market carried out in the countryside by peasants and artisans before the coming of modern factories[cite: 16].
  • Coming of Factories: Cotton emerged as the leading sector in the late 18th century, followed by iron and steel; Richard Arkwright created the cotton mill[cite: 16].
  • Hand Labour vs. Machines: In Victorian Britain, abundant cheap labour meant industrialists preferred manual labour for seasonal work and intricate designs demanded by the upper classes[cite: 16].
  • Life of Workers: Dependent on friendship networks, plagued by seasonality of unemployment, leading to hostility against new technologies like the Spinning Jenny[cite: 16].

II. Industrialisation in India & Factories

Indian Industrial Aspects Overview
Decline of Weavers East India Company established monopoly control, appointed paid servants (*gomasthas*), and used advances to bind weavers, destroying traditional export markets[cite: 16].
Manchester Imports Cheap machine-made Manchester cloth flooded India in the 19th century, crippling local handloom weavers and cutting off raw material supplies during the American Civil War[cite: 16].
Rise of Indian Mills First cotton mill in Bombay (1854) and jute mill in Bengal (1855); entrepreneurs built fortunes through China trade (Dwarkanath Tagore, J.N. Tata)[cite: 16].

III. Peculiarities of Industrial Growth & Markets

Small-scale industries predominated in India, with handloom production expanding in the 20th century through the adoption of the fly shuttle[cite: 16]. Manufacturers expanded markets using advertisements, labels featuring Indian gods and goddesses, calendars, and Swadeshi nationalist messaging[cite: 16].
