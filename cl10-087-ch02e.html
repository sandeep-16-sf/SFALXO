Chapter 2: Sectors of the Indian Economy

Class 10 Economics Notes

I. Sectors of Economic Activities

Economic activities are categorized based on the nature of activity into primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors, which are highly interdependent[cite: 11].
  • Primary Sector: Activities undertaken by directly exploiting natural resources (e.g., agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry)[cite: 11].
  • Secondary Sector: Covers activities where natural products are changed into manufactured forms through industrial processes[cite: 11].
  • Tertiary Sector (Service Sector): Generates services that support primary and secondary sectors, alongside essential services like banking, transport, and IT[cite: 11].

II. Comparing Sectors & Employment Challenges

Concept Key Details
GDP / GVA Gross Domestic Product (and Gross Value Added) represents the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a year[cite: 11].
Disguised Unemployment A situation of underemployment where people appear to work, but their labor effort is divided and their potential is underutilized (common in agriculture)[cite: 11].
Employment Guarantee Measures like MGNREGA / Viksit Bharat-G RAM G guarantee 100 days of wage employment in rural areas[cite: 11].

III. Organised vs Unorganised & Public vs Private Sectors

Organised Sector: Enterprises with regular employment terms, job security, fixed working hours, and government regulation (e.g., factories act)[cite: 11].
Unorganised Sector: Small, scattered units outside strict government control featuring low pay, lack of security, and absence of paid leave[cite: 11].
Public vs. Private Sectors: Public sector assets are owned by the government with a primary focus on social welfare, while private sector entities are guided by profit motives[cite: 11].
