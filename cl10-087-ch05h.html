Chapter 5: Print Culture and the Modern World

Class 10 History Notes

I. First Printed Books & The Print Revolution in Europe

Print Revolution: A transformation in human society where the printing press shifted reading from an elite privilege to a mass culture, altering how information and authority were shared[cite: 17].
  • East Asian Origins: Hand-printing technology developed in China, Japan, and Korea using woodblocks; the oldest printed book is the Buddhist Diamond Sutra (AD 868)[cite: 17].
  • Gutenberg's Press: Johann Gutenberg developed the movable metal-type printing press in Strasbourg in the 1430s, printing his famous Bible in the 1450s[cite: 17].
  • Religious Debates: Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses (1517) challenged the Roman Catholic Church, sparking the Protestant Reformation aided by print[cite: 17].

II. Reading Mania, French Revolution & Print in India

Key Themes Overview
French Revolution Print popularized Enlightenment ideas, fostered a culture of debate, and circulated satirical literature against the monarchy[cite: 17].
Print Comes to India Began with Portuguese missionaries in Goa (mid-16th century); James Augustus Hickey launched the first English newspaper, Bengal Gazette, in 1780[cite: 17].
Religious Reform & Public Debates Intense debates on social issues (Rammohun Roy's Sambad Kaumudi, Deoband fatwas, vernacular religious texts)[cite: 17].

III. Women, Print and Censorship

Print empowered women to write and read about their experiences (Rashsundari Debi's Amar Jiban, Tarabai Shinde). Poor workers accessed literature and wrote about caste and class exploitation (Jyotiba Phule's Gulamgiri, Kashibaba)[cite: 17]. Growing nationalist press led to colonial repression, notably the stringent Vernacular Press Act of 1878[cite: 17].
