Water Resources

Complete Class 10 Geography Notes

I. Water Scarcity and Need for Conservation

Although 3/4th of the earth's surface is covered with water, freshwater accounts for a very small proportion. Water scarcity is caused by over-exploitation, excessive use, and unequal access among different social groups[cite: 5].

Rapid industrialization, urbanisation, and growing agricultural demands have further exerted pressure on existing freshwater resources[cite: 5].

II. Multi-Purpose River Projects

Dams are barriers across flowing water that obstruct, direct, or retard flow, creating reservoirs[cite: 5]. Jawaharlal Nehru proudly proclaimed dams as the "Temples of Modern India" because they integrate agricultural development with rapid industrial growth[cite: 5].

Criticism of Dams: Regulating rivers affects natural flow, causes excessive sedimentation, submerges vegetation, fragments aquatic life migration, and can induce earthquakes[cite: 5].

III. Rainwater Harvesting Systems

  • Guls and Kuls: Diversion channels in Western Himalayas for agriculture[cite: 5].
  • Tankas: Underground tanks in arid/semi-arid regions of Rajasthan (e.g., Bikaner, Phalodi) to store drinking water (palar pani)[cite: 5].
  • Bamboo Drip Irrigation: A 200-year-old system in Meghalaya of tapping stream/spring water using bamboo pipes[cite: 5].
