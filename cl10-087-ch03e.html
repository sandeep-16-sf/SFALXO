Chapter 3: Money and Credit

Class 10 Economics Notes

I. Money as a Medium of Exchange

Money acts as an intermediate in exchange processes, eliminating the need for a "double coincidence of wants" required in barter systems[cite: 10].
  • Modern Currency: Consists of paper notes and coins authorized by the government and issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the central government[cite: 10].
  • Bank Deposits: People deposit extra cash into bank accounts, creating demand deposits which can be withdrawn on demand and utilized for payment via cheques[cite: 10].

II. Loan Activities and Terms of Credit

Banking / Credit Element Description
Bank Intermediation Banks keep a small cash reserve (approx. 5%) and use the rest of deposits to extend loans, earning profit from the interest rate differential[cite: 10].
Terms of Credit Includes interest rate, collateral (security asset like land titles or deposits), documentation requirements, and mode of repayment[cite: 10].

III. Formal vs Informal Credit & Self-Help Groups (SHGs)

Formal Sources: Banks and cooperatives offering cheap credit monitored by the RBI[cite: 10].
Informal Sources: Moneylenders, traders, and relatives charging exorbitant interest rates without regulation, often trapping borrowers in debt[cite: 10].
Self-Help Groups: Small groups (15-20 members) of rural poor (mostly women) who pool savings and provide collateral-free internal loans, fostering financial self-reliance[cite: 10].
