Agriculture

Complete Class 10 Geography Notes

I. Types of Farming

Agriculture is an age-old economic activity in India; two-thirds of its population is engaged in agricultural activities[cite: 4].

Subsistence Farming

Practised on small patches with primitive tools and family labour. Includes Primitive Subsistence (Slash and Burn / Jhumming) and Intensive Subsistence Farming[cite: 4].

Commercial Farming

Uses higher doses of modern inputs (HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers). Plantation is a type where a single crop is grown on a large area (e.g., tea, coffee, rubber)[cite: 4].

II. Cropping Seasons in India

  • Rabi Crops: Sown in winter (Oct-Dec), harvested in summer (Apr-Jun). Major crops: wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard[cite: 4].
  • Kharif Crops: Grown with onset of monsoon, harvested in Sep-Oct. Major crops: paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur, cotton, jute, soybean[cite: 4].
  • Zaid Season: Short summer season between rabi and kharif. Crops: watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, fodder[cite: 4].

III. Major Crops & Technological Reforms

India is a leading producer of rice, wheat, millets, pulses, sugarcane, tea, coffee, oilseeds, cotton, and jute[cite: 4]. Technological and institutional reforms include the Green Revolution, White Revolution, Land Reforms, Bhoodan-Gramdan movement, and Kisan Credit Card (KCC)[cite: 4].

Quick Revision

Jhumming

Slash and burn agriculture known as Milpa in Mexico, Roca in Brazil, and Jhumming in NE India[cite: 4].

Cropping Seasons

Rabi (winter), Kharif (monsoon), and Zaid (summer)[cite: 4].

Staple Crop

Rice is the staple food crop; India is the 2nd largest producer after China[cite: 4].

Blood-less Revolution

Bhoodan-Gramdan movement initiated by Vinoba Bhave[cite: 4].
