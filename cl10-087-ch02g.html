I. Biodiversity and Conservation in India

Biodiversity refers to the immense richness of wildlife and cultivated species, diverse in form and function, closely integrated through multiple networks of interdependencies[cite: 6].

Conservation preserves ecological diversity, life support systems (water, air, soil), and genetic diversity[cite: 6]. The Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act was implemented in 1972[cite: 6].

Project Tiger: Launched in 1973 to protect dwindling tiger populations from poaching, habitat shrinking, and illegal trade[cite: 6].

II. Classification of Forests

Forest Category Description & Significance
Reserved Forests More than half of total forest land; regarded as most valuable for wildlife and forest resource conservation[cite: 6].
Protected Forests Almost one-third of total forest area; protected from further depletion by the Forest Department[cite: 6].
Unclassed Forests Other forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals/communities[cite: 6].

III. Community and Conservation

  • Chipko Movement: Successfully resisted deforestation in the Himalayas and proved community afforestation works[cite: 6].
  • Sacred Groves: Virgin forests left untouched by local tribal communities as forests of Gods and Goddesses[cite: 6].
  • Joint Forest Management (JFM): Involves local village institutions in restoring degraded forest lands, initiated by Odisha in 1988[cite: 6].
