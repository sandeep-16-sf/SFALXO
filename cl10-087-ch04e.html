Chapter 4: Globalisation and the Indian Economy

Class 10 Economics Notes

I. Production Across Countries & MNCs

Globalisation: The process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries driven by foreign trade and foreign investments[cite: 9].
  • Multinational Corporations (MNCs): Companies that own or control production in more than one nation, setting up offices and factories where labor and resources are cheap[cite: 9].
  • Interlinking Production: MNCs spread production globally by collaborating with local companies, buying local firms, or placing orders with small producers[cite: 9].

II. Factors Enabling Globalisation & Trade Barriers

Enabling Factor / Policy Impact
Technology Rapid improvements in transport (containers) and Information and Communication Technology (IT, internet, e-banking) facilitate global operations[cite: 9].
Liberalisation Removal of government-imposed trade barriers or restrictions on foreign trade and investment, significantly introduced in India in 1991[cite: 9].
World Trade Organisation (WTO) An international body promoting free trade, though often criticized for unequal power dynamics between developed and developing nations[cite: 9].

III. Impact of Globalisation & Fair Globalisation

While well-off consumers and skilled producers benefit from lower prices and greater choices, small producers face stiff competition, and workers face flexible, insecure employment conditions[cite: 9]. Fair globalisation aims to create equal opportunities and ensure benefits are shared equitably among all sections of society[cite: 9].
