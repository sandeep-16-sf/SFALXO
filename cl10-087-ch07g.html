I. Means of Transport: Roadways & Railways

Movement of goods and services from supply locations to demand locations necessitates transport, communication, and trade, which are complementary to each other[cite: 1].
    Roadways: India has the second-largest road network in the world. Roads are classified into Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways, National Highways, State Highways, District Roads, Other Roads (Pradhan Mantri Grameen Sadak Yojana), and Border Roads[cite: 1].
    Railways: Principal mode of transportation for freight and passengers in India for over 150 years. First train ran from Mumbai to Thane in 1853 (34 km)[cite: 1].

II. Pipelines, Waterways, and Airways

Transport Domain Key Features & Networks
Pipelines Used for transporting crude oil, petroleum products, natural gas, and solids (as slurry). Example: Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur (HVJ) pipeline[cite: 1].
Waterways Cheapest means of transport; suitable for heavy and bulky goods. Includes National Waterways No. 1, 2, 3, etc[cite: 1].
Airways Fastest, most comfortable, and prestigious mode of transport. Reaches difficult terrains easily[cite: 1].

III. Communication and International Trade

Communication is divided into personal and mass communication (radio, television, newspapers, films)[cite: 1]. International trade (exchange of goods between countries) serves as an economic barometer for a country[cite: 1]. The balance of trade is the difference between exports and imports (favourable when exports exceed imports)[cite: 1].
