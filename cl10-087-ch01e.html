Chapter 1: Development

Class 10 Economics Notes

I. What Development Promises & National Development

Development involves thinking about individual aspirations, national progress, and working towards goals like better income, equality, freedom, security, and respect[cite: 12]. Different people can have different or even conflicting developmental goals[cite: 12].
  • Different People, Different Goals: Different categories of persons (e.g., landless laborers, prosperous farmers, urban unemployed) have distinct and sometimes conflicting developmental goals[cite: 12].
  • Income and Other Goals: While material things and higher income are important, the quality of life also depends on non-material aspects like equal treatment, freedom, security, and a pollution-free environment[cite: 12].
  • National Development: Involves finding a fair and just path that benefits a large number of people rather than a small group[cite: 12].

II. Comparing Countries and Public Facilities

Indicator / Tool Description & Usage
Average Income (Per Capita Income) Total income of a country divided by its total population. Used by the World Bank to classify rich and low-income countries, though it hides disparities[cite: 12].
Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) Indicates the number of children that die before the age of one year per 1,000 live births[cite: 12].
Human Development Index (HDI) Published by the UNDP, it compares countries based on per capita income, education levels, and health status (life expectancy)[cite: 12].

III. Sustainability of Development

Sustainability focuses on maintaining or improving the level of development for future generations without causing environmental degradation[cite: 12]. It distinguishes between renewable resources (like groundwater, which can be overused if consumed faster than nature replenishes them) and non-renewable resources (like crude oil, which have fixed stocks and will eventually get exhausted)[cite: 12].
